About

I am a Principal Mathematical Statistician in the Center for Statistical Research and Methodology at the U.S. Census Bureau.

My research develops statistical methods for official statistics and complex data integration, with particular emphasis on survey sampling and variance estimation, record linkage and mismatch error, interpretable machine learning, spatial modeling, and graphical and Bayesian models.

A recurring theme in my work is connecting methodological theory with practical problems involving survey operations, linked data, nonresponse, prediction, and data quality. I received my Ph.D. in Statistics from Indiana University and completed postdoctoral research at SAMSI and Stanford University.

02

Research

My work spans mathematical statistics and applied methodology, organized around four closely connected themes.

01

Survey Sampling & Official Statistics

Variance estimation, nonresponse adjustment, response-rate modeling, and statistical methods that support reliable public data products.

02

Linked Data & Record Linkage

Valid inference when linked records contain mismatch error, including regression, uncertainty quantification, and robust secondary analysis.

03

Statistical Machine Learning

Interpretable prediction, sparse modeling, structured interactions, and spatial methods for complex operational and survey data.

04

Graphical & Bayesian Models

Gaussian graphical and directed acyclic graph models, covariance geometry, high-dimensional inference, and structured Bayesian methods.
